# Abraham Accords Expansion in 2025

## Recent Developments

**Kazakhstan has formally joined the Abraham Accords in 2025.** On November 6, 2025, Kazakhstan became the fifth country to officially accede to the agreements, announced during a White House meeting between Kazakh President Kassym-Jomart Tokayev and US President Donald Trump at the C5+1 summit involving Central Asian leaders.[2][3] This marks a significant expansion of the accords beyond their original Middle Eastern and North African focus.

## Current Signatories

The Abraham Accords now include five countries:[2][4]

- United Arab Emirates
- Bahrain
- Morocco
- Sudan
- Kazakhstan

Kazakhstan's addition is particularly notable because, despite having maintained full diplomatic relations with Israel since 1992 and existing bilateral agreements, its formal accession to the accords represents a symbolic broadening of the framework to encompass Central Asian nations.[3][4]

## Countries Under Serious Consideration

**Syria** stands out as a major candidate for future membership. Following the toppling of Bashar al-Assad's regime in December 2024, Syria's new leader Ahmed al-Sharaa has signaled willingness to engage with Israel diplomatically, opening unprecedented opportunities for normalization discussions that began in 2025.[4] Additionally, **Saudi Arabia** has shown serious interest in joining the accords, a development that would carry significant geopolitical weight given its regional prominence.[4]

## Strategic Context and Timing

The Trump administration has made expansion of the Abraham Accords a policy priority. The timing of Kazakhstan's announcement was strategic—occurring during the C5+1 summit and positioned ahead of Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman's scheduled November 18 visit to Washington, suggesting momentum-building efforts.[3] US officials have emphasized that the accords represent "a club that many countries want to be a member of" and serve as a platform for demonstrating international support for Israel while promoting regional cooperation and religious tolerance.[1][3]

The **October 2025 Israel-Hamas ceasefire** has revived broader discussions about expanding the accords, creating diplomatic space for new signatories to engage without the complication of active major conflict.[4]

## Expert Assessment

Analysts acknowledge that while Kazakhstan's accession holds symbolic value demonstrating the accords' geographic expansion, experts remain divided on its practical impact. Daniel B. Shapiro characterized it as "no great breakthrough, but it holds some symbolic value," while Sarah Zaaimi noted that "a broader pan-Abrahamic bloc is forming" with potential to expand beyond the Middle East to encompass strategically important Muslim-majority nations across Asia, Africa, and Eurasia.[5]

The expansion reflects a shift in the accords' mission from exclusively Arab-Israeli normalization to a broader multilateral framework focused on commerce, regional cooperation, and geopolitical alignment with US interests.[5]
